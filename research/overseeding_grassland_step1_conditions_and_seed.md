# Overseeding grassland – Step 1: conditions, soil type and seed choice

*Literature review, Dutch and English sources (WUR / PPS Ruwvoer & Bodem, Louis Bolk Instituut, Praktijkonderzoek Rundvee, Teagasc (IE), SAC/FAS (UK), trade journals). Compiled 19 October 2026.*

---

## 1. Key message in five points

1. **Moisture is the deciding factor.** In all studies, failed overseedings are almost always due to drought. Only sow into a moist, workable top layer (moisture in at least the top 5 cm), preferably just before expected rain.
2. **The old sward is the competitor.** A dense, closed sward leaves little room. Short mowing or grazing down, harrowing and a lower N dose after sowing decide whether the seedlings survive.
3. **Seed-to-soil contact.** Rolling and well-closed slits made the difference between 3–10 % and 46 % emergence in the WUR trial (see §6).
4. **Timing.** In the Netherlands, late August and September are the most favourable for grass (October can still work, as long as the soil stays above about 8–10 °C). For clover, spring or early autumn is safer. In Ireland and Scotland spring (April–May) is preferred.
5. **Plan for repetition.** One overseeding rarely produces a large change at once. Overseeding is maintenance, not a replacement for reseeding a truly worn-out sward.

---

## 2. Overseeding or reseeding?

| | Overseeding | Reseeding |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | approx. € 100–150/ha (melkvee.nl) | much higher (ploughing, seedbed, loss of production) |
| Soil | sward, organic matter, soil life and soil C stay intact | loss of C and N, soil life disturbed |
| Success rate | lower: poorer seedbed, weather-dependent, competition from the old sward, no levelling | higher and more predictable |
| Suitable for | reasonable sward (≥ 60 % good grasses), bare patches, introducing clover or new cultivars | worn-out sward, much weed or creeping grass, "butty swards" |

- Teagasc and SAC: on old swards with little perennial ryegrass, **do not** overseed; reseed instead.
- WUR: overseeding into a closed sward with good cover "seems little promising". Open patches are needed.

## 3. Soil type

| Soil type | Findings | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Clay** | Overseeding of both grass and clover worked best on clay (PR 1995–96). Clover on clay was better than on sand with all machines. PPS trial on clay (Friesland, 2016–2019): perennial ryegrass +5 to +7 percentage points after a successful overseeding. Target pH about 6. | WUR edepot 47642, 562083 |
| **Sand** | Greatest risk from drying out. Overseeding in **May** on sand was clearly worse than in March or August, unless irrigated. Combined with sward fertiliser, emergence on sand was poor, especially for clover. Target pH about 5.5. On poor sand, extra white clover in the mix (e.g. 3 kg/ha) can help. | PR 97-1; melkvee.nl |
| **Peat** | Little specific research found. A Ekoland practice network tested machines on peat but did not publish success figures. Points of attention: peat stays moist for a long time (favourable), but has low bearing capacity and the sward is often dense and felty. Target pH about 5. | boerenbusiness.nl; Ekoland |
| **Stream-side soil (moist sand)** | Louis Bolk Institute, Aver Heino: clover overseeding with the Hunter strip-till machine on 17 May, with 30 % clover cover after 2 months and 75 % after just over 3 months. In the following year a yield equal to reseeding. | Prins 1997 |

**Soil fertility** (all sources): correct pH, P and K before overseeding.

- Teagasc for clover: pH 6.5, P and K index ≥ 3. Liming preferably one season earlier.
- Dutch sources for grass: pH 5–6, depending on soil type. White clover minimum pH 5.0 (Liflex advice), higher is better.

## 4. Timing, temperature and moisture

### 4.1 Soil temperature

| Threshold | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| ~8 °C | fastest perennial ryegrass cultivars start to germinate; do not sow below 8 °C | Barenbrug via Boerenbusiness |
| ~10 °C | "safe average"; clover: 10 °C at 10 cm depth | Boerenbusiness; FWI |
| > 12 °C | slower cultivars and supplier guideline | Barenbrug cultivation handbook |
| Continuous night temperature < 10 °C | stop overseeding | WUR Factsheet Ruwvoer & Bodem 9 |
| ~6 °C | growth limit of Italian/hybrid mixture (Supersward) | Cotswold Seeds |

**Measured in the PPS trial (average temperature 14 days after sowing):** 16.1 °C (2016), 13.6 °C (2017), 14.9 °C (2018), 14.2 °C (2019). Temperature therefore did not explain the differences. Moisture and closing the slits did.

### 4.2 Moisture

- **Wet enough and dry enough:** the top layer must be workable. If the top layer is *too dry*, the machine cuts poorly and the seed dries out. If the top layer is *too wet*, the slit smears shut and seedlings are lost (2017 was too wet).
- After sowing, moisture must remain until emergence. In 2018, 10 mm was irrigated twice (8 and 13 days after sowing).
- Louis Bolk: of the failed attempts in 1994–1996, "almost all" were due to drought.
- PR 1996: after sowing in May it became dry and many seedlings dried out shortly after emergence.

### 4.3 Calendar

| Period | Grass (perennial ryegrass) | Clover | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **March (early spring)** | fair on sand (PR) / **not** according to PPS (start of the growing season, old sward grows too fast) | good: clover can often start in mid-March | conflicting advice: PR 1996 found March good, PPS 2020 advises against early spring |
| **April–May** | moderate: old sward competes strongly, drought risk (especially on sand) | **best period** according to Teagasc and SAC (IE/UK climate) | in May: a pre-cut of 3–4 t DM/ha and short mowing to limit competition |
| **After 1st cut (mid-May)** | variable | good with sufficient moisture (Aver Heino) | slug damage is greatest in spring |
| **Late Aug.–Sept.** | **best period in NL**: warm soil, often moisture, less weed, old sward grows more slowly | possible, but lower success rate; risk of too little development before winter | PR: clover after August is probably too late |
| **October** | can still work if soil > 8 °C and no night frost | not advisable | PPS sowed between 15 Sept. and 4 Oct. |

## 5. Seed species and mixtures

### 5.1 Perennial ryegrass (*Lolium perenne*): the basis for permanent grassland

- **Tetraploid** germinates faster, has larger seed and establishes itself more "aggressively" among existing grass (supplier claim). Many overseeding mixtures are 70 % tetraploid and 30 % diploid (TopGrass Overseeding), or even 100 % tetraploid.
- **Diploid** gives a denser sward and better trafficability, but establishes more slowly.
- PPS trial: tetraploid cv. Dromara, 25 kg/ha.

### 5.2 Italian ryegrass (*Lolium multiflorum*) and hybrid ryegrass (*L. × hybridum*)

- **Advantage:** larger seed, more germinative vigour and faster establishment, so a better chance in an existing sward. A UK adviser says: "Italians and hybrids are larger seeded and have greater germinative vigour".
- **Disadvantage:** short-lived (Italian 1–2 years, hybrid 2–4 years), less persistent and less winter-hardy. An old WUR report names winter kill in harsh winters as a major risk.
- **Use:** a quick repair after drought or pest damage, or a temporary production boost. The example mixture is 70 % tetraploid Italian + 30 % hybrid, 25 kg/ha. For permanent grassland the Dutch sources recommend perennial ryegrass, because Italian does not improve the sward in the long run.
- In an old WUR study, spring overseeding of Italian ryegrass gave the highest annual yield of the treatments tested.

### 5.3 White clover (*Trifolium repens*)

- **Seed rate:** 5–6 kg/ha (Teagasc, SAC), in the Netherlands often 3–5 kg/ha in a mixture with grass.
- **Leaf size:** small-leaved for intensive and continuous grazing (sheep), medium-leaved for cattle and rotational paddocks, large-leaved for cutting.
  - Teagasc trial: AberHerald + AberSwan (medium).
  - Netherlands: Liflex, Alice; Quartet as a large-leaved cultivar.
- **Sowing depth:** about 10 mm. Very small seed, so not deeper.
- **More difficult than grass:** the success rate with overseeding is lower, clover is sensitive to slugs, light (shading) and N fertiliser.
- **Advantage:** a small number of plants can still give a good cover via stolons.

### 5.4 Red clover and herbs

- Red clover can be added in September when overseeding, but its success rate is lower than for grass. Herbs (chicory, narrow-leaved plantain) are very sensitive to competition. The Drenthe factsheet on herb-rich grassland advises reseeding rather than overseeding.

### 5.5 Seed rates summarised

| Purpose | Rate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Periodic maintenance (perennial ryegrass) | 10–15 kg/ha | Barenbrug / Boerenbusiness |
| Minimum for an effect | ≥ 15 kg/ha good, high-germination seed | WUR Factsheet 9 |
| Repair after drought or damage | 20–25 kg/ha | Barenbrug |
| Trial PPS / PR | 25 kg/ha (PPS); 20 kg/ha grass or 5 kg/ha clover (PR) | WUR |
| White clover overseeding | 5–6 kg/ha (untreated seed) | Teagasc, SAC |
| Italian/hybrid short-term mixture | 25 kg/ha | Cotswold Seeds |

**Sowing depth grass:** about twice the seed length, so usually **12–15 mm** (WUR). PR: never deeper than 3–4 cm.

## 6. Successes: what the figures show

### PPS Ruwvoer & Bodem, 5 clay plots (Dairy Campus), 2016–2019, Vredo Agri, 25 kg/ha

| Year (sowing date) | Conditions | Seedlings/m² | Emergence of sown seed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 (15 Sept.) | 10 days dry before and 15 days dry after sowing | 28 | 3 % |
| 2017 (4 Oct.) | too wet, more irregular seed distribution | 30 | 3 % |
| 2018 (3 Oct.) | good top layer, 2 × 10 mm irrigated | 84 | 10 % |
| 2019 (18 Sept.) | ideal top layer **+ extra rolling, slits fully closed** | 418 | **46 %** |

- 868–958 seeds/m² were sown.
- Overseeding in 2018 raised the share of perennial ryegrass in the summer of 2019 by **5–7 percentage points** (63 → 68 %).
- After 2019 the effect disappeared because of heavy vole damage.
- There was **no** effect on grass yield. There was a higher feed value, however: higher VEM and sugar content (+8 %).
- More open slits meant more seedlings: 1 percentage point less cover gave 8–20 extra seedlings/m².
- Harrowing was sufficient against rough meadow-grass. Chemical control added little.

### Louis Bolk / Hunter strip-till (organic, 1994–96)

- White clover in perennial ryegrass gave 75 % clover cover after just over 3 months and a yield equal to reseeding in the year after.

### Teagasc Johnstown Castle (clover, 27 April, 5 kg/ha)

- Seedlings per m²: **direct drill (Erth) 591**, harrow + pneumatic seeder (Einböck) 507, **fertiliser spreader 202**.

### Teagasc Ballyhaise (heavy soil)

- 2021: 80 % of the overseeded area succeeded, with mid-40 % clover in Aug./Sept.
- 2022: completely failed because of wrong timing.
- 2023: skipped (first too wet, then quickly dry).

## 7. Challenges and failure factors

| Failure factor | What happens | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| **Drought** | seedlings dry out shortly after emergence | sow before rain; irrigate (2 × 10 mm); roll well |
| **Too wet** | slits smear shut, poor distribution, wheel tracks | postpone |
| **Competition from the old sward** | the sward closes within a few weeks and suppresses the seedlings | mow short (to about 2–4 cm) or graze down; harrow; reduce N (PPS: no N at the 5th cut; Teagasc: 15 kg N/ha per round, 2 rounds); graze in time (about 1000 kg DM/ha, first time after about 18 days to 5 weeks) |
| **Felt layer / dead material** | no seed-to-soil contact | harrow vigorously beforehand and remove the material |
| **Slugs** | halving of seedlings after 1 month (436 vs 232/m²), clover cover 69 vs 32 % | slug pellets (spring) |
| **Voles** | effect of a successful overseeding (2019) completely gone | vole control; overseeded plots had less damage |
| **Weeds** | slow establishment on sand, weeds win | control beforehand (late summer); watch herbicide residues (up to 4 months) |
| **Poor soil fertility** | slow, especially for clover | get pH, P and K right beforehand |
| **Machine settings** | poor cutting, wrong depth, poor distribution, open slits | sow and check a test strip (see Step 2) |
| **Judging too early** | clover may only be clearly visible after 1.5–2 years | do not overseed or harrow again immediately |

## 8. Practical checklist (synthesis of the sources)

**Beforehand**
- [ ] Assess the sward: ≥ 60 % good grasses? Open patches? Otherwise reseed.
- [ ] Soil sample: correct pH, P, K (clover: pH ~6.5).
- [ ] Control weeds and rough meadow-grass (late summer, 2 weeks before overseeding). For clover use only clover-safe products.

**Timing**
- [ ] Grass: late August – September (October until soil < 8–10 °C). Clover: April–May or early August.
- [ ] Top layer workable and moist at 0–5 cm. Rain expected. No night frost.

**Execution**
- [ ] Mow or graze as short as possible, directly after a cut.
- [ ] Harrow vigorously, remove dead material.
- [ ] ≥ 15 kg/ha perennial ryegrass (tetraploid-rich), or 5–6 kg/ha white clover. 20–25 kg/ha for damage repair.
- [ ] Depth: grass 12–15 mm, clover ~10 mm.
- [ ] Test strip: cutting, depth, distribution, closing of the slits.
- [ ] Rolling (Cambridge or extra pressure roller), slits closed.

**Aftercare**
- [ ] Limit N fertiliser to establishment. Graze at ~1000 kg DM/ha to give light.
- [ ] Watch slugs and voles.
- [ ] Count seedlings after 3–6 weeks (target: hundreds of plants/m²).

---

## 9. Knowledge gaps

- **Peat soil:** no quantitative Dutch trial results found.
- **Italian ryegrass in an existing sward:** no direct comparison of emergence speed with perennial ryegrass found. The advantages mainly come from supplier sources.
- Many figures come from a few trial years or one location. They are therefore indicative, not directly comparable.
